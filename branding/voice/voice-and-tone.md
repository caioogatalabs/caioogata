# Voice and tone

How the personal brand sounds. Companion to [`who-is-caio.md`](who-is-caio.md), which
covers *what* is said; this covers *how*.

Rescued 2026-09-10 from `.archive/legacy-docs/CONTENT.md` §2, where it had been sitting
unread inside a frozen 1,469-line V1 document. The principles survive intact — they are
good, and they already prescribed the plain-language rewrite we did. What changed is the
reader.

---

## Who is reading

**Now: someone deciding whether to hire Caio for a piece of work.** Often not a designer.
Often not technical. They want to know what he makes, whether he can do the whole thing,
and whether he is a safe bet.

**Before: recruiters and hiring managers.** The old guide named "tech-savvy recruiters
(28–45)" and "hiring managers in design/tech leadership" as the audience, and the site's
copy was written to match. That is why it reads like a credential list.

This single change invalidates more of the old guide than any of the principles do. A line
that works on a recruiter — *"open to remote work with the right opportunity"* — is dead
weight on a client.

---

## Principles

**Clarity above all**
- Simple language, even for complex ideas
- If a term needs jargon, explain it immediately — or cut the sentence
- Sentences max 20–25 words
- One idea per paragraph

**Human and accessible**
- Write like an intelligent person talking to another
- Casual but professional. Never corporate-speak
- No marketing hyperbole: *game-changing*, *revolutionary*, *disruptive*, *passionate*
- Active voice. "Led the design team", not "the design team was led"

**Honest**
- Don't inflate. Claim the ceiling the facts support, not one step above
- Concrete example beats vague claim, every time
- Admit the edges when they matter

**Useful**
- Say what the reader gets, not only what Caio did
- One clear next step
- Remove friction

## Tone by context

Same voice everywhere. The register moves.

| Where | Register |
|---|---|
| **caioogata.com** | Plain, direct, third person. Selling creative work to a client who may not be technical. No jargon survives here. |
| **COLab** (`branding/templates/commercial/`) | The company, in PT, first person plural. Sells products and delivery. More formal, more commercial. |
| **CVs and cover letters** (`career/`) | Recruiter-facing, and that is fine — this is the one place the credential vocabulary belongs. First person. |
| **Articles, reels, posts** (`content/`) | Loosest. Caio thinking out loud. First person, longer leash. |

Do not let the registers leak. The site drifting into CV vocabulary is exactly the problem
this rewrite is fixing.

## Words to avoid on the site

Not banned everywhere — banned on `caioogata.com`, because the reader there does not share
the vocabulary.

`design systems` · `developer experience` · `design engineering` · `end-to-end ownership` ·
`at the intersection of` · `leverage` · `passionate` · `innovative` · `cutting-edge` ·
`seamless` · `robust` · `synergy`

Every term on the first line is real and describes real work. They belong in a CV and in a
conversation with another designer. On the site they cost the reader more than they pay.

## Yes

From the approved copy in [`who-is-caio.md`](who-is-caio.md):

> Creative Designer who learned to build.

> Twenty years in advertising, fifteen in interfaces, and a self-taught path into code.

> Most people who do this work do one part of it. Caio does the span. The idea, the way it
> looks, and the thing that actually runs.

> Interaction and motion are not decoration added to a finished layout.

Why these work: a plain noun, a real number, a named contrast, a sentence you could say out
loud.

## No

**Marketing inflation**
> I'm a passionate, innovative design leader leveraging cutting-edge methodologies

**Gimmick**
> I'm a rockstar ninja guru

**Arrogance**
> Obviously, my design systems revolutionize workflows

**Redundancy**
> Simply just click here

**Taxonomy instead of a sentence** — the site's own headline until 2026-09
> Design Director · Design Systems & Developer Experience · Design Engineering

**Job-seeking on a sales page** — these were "yes" examples in the old guide, and they are
correct for a CV. On the site they answer a question the client is not asking.
> I work best with teams that value iteration over perfection

> Based in Porto Alegre, open to remote work with the right opportunity

## Voice characteristics

Carried over from the original guide. These describe how Caio actually sounds, so they
survive the change of audience.

**Precision** — few wasted words. Results before adjectives. Comfortable talking about
process, training, iteration.

**Technical literacy** — at ease with developer vocabulary, and able to drop it. Names tools
and methods naturally when the reader shares them, never to signal.

**Depth through analogy** — sport and physical discipline as the way into an idea. Judo,
skateboarding, learning by falling. Genuine, and the strongest human material the brand
owns.

*On that last one:* it is deliberately absent from the approved bio, because the bio has to
sell and a client does not need his sports history in the first 250 words. It belongs
somewhere with more room — a philosophy section, an interview, a page about how he works.
The material is preserved in `who-is-caio.md` under "How he works".
