# Who is Caio Ogata

Base document for the **personal brand**. Everything the site says about Caio derives from
here. When a fact or a phrasing conflicts somewhere else, this file wins.

Not the same as COLab. COLab is the company and sells products and delivery. This is the
person, selling creative work directly. Same human, different pitch. See
[open-decisions](#open-decisions) for what still needs Caio's call.

Written English-first; the PT version is a translation, not a separate text.

## The line

> ### Creative Designer who learned to build.
>
> Twenty years in advertising, fifteen in interfaces, and a self-taught path into code.

The title names the crossing. The complement carries the seniority without naming a job
title, and settles what "build" means — `code` removes the ambiguity (building a team? a
brand?), `interfaces` is the bridge word that connects the advertising years to the software
years. Drop either and the arc has a hole in the middle.

Where there is more room, open with the same identity and develop it toward the outcome:
taking work from idea to production.

**What this replaces:** `Design Director · Design Systems & Developer Experience · Design
Engineering` — three disciplines separated by dots. A filing label, not a sentence. Written
for LinkedIn and for recruiters; the site has a different job, which is to win work.

deciding whether to hire meets the work before the résumé. Paragraphs 1–2 are who he is and
how he works; 3 is where the craft comes from; 4–5 are the two halves of the record; 6 is
the only paragraph that says what the reader gets.

**What it drops on purpose:** design systems, developer experience, design engineering,

has to sell. It stays available for a philosophy section elsewhere.

**Experience and skills are not this bio's job.** The site carries them in their own
sections. This text stops at the point where a curious reader would go look.

## Where he comes from

He started in advertising, as an art director. Agencies, campaigns, brands.

Then a studio — Huia, in Porto Alegre. He joined at the beginning and stayed nine years,
becoming a partner and head of creative. The studio grew from a handful of people to around
forty and was acquired by Stefanini. He led creative direction on 40+ digital projects for
Petrobras, O Boticário, Tramontina, Sicredi, Aché, and international work through Mondelez
brands — Lacta, Bis, Toblerone, Oreo.

Then product. Azion Technologies hired him as Design Director to build a design operation
from the ground up, inside an engineering-first company — a different brief from Huia,
where he had grown with the studio. He was not the company's first designer; the job was to
structure the discipline. He built it to fourteen people across product design, creative
design, design ops, UX research and design systems, and created azion.design, the public
design system documentation. Over four years he moved through three roles — Design
Director, then Brand Experience Director, then Developer Experience Director — leading the
company's rebrand and ending with the entire user-facing engineering layer: console, CLI,
developer tools, website, documentation.

The thing he points at as proof is the Azion Console Kit: a full front-end rebuild of a
cloud platform's management interface, open source, in production at Itaú, Magazine Luiza
and Netshoes. Vue 3, TypeScript, headless components. It cut new-screen implementation from
days to hours. 6,000+ commits, 34+ contributors.

Now he works for himself.

## How he works

He learned this on a judo mat, and it is still the clearest thing he has said about his own
work:

> In Judo, the first lessons are all focused on teaching how to fall. From the beginning,
> it's understood that you will fall, lose, err. So suffer less, lose the fear, preserve
> your body and, most importantly, prepare your mind for the obstacles ahead.
>
> — Caio, 2018

Skateboarding taught him the same thing. Skaters fall hundreds of times learning one trick
and do it without fear, because falling is the process, not a failure of it.

That is how he builds. Ship early, get it in front of people, change it. Nothing good
arrives finished.

*(Deliberately **not** in the bio. It reads as philosophy, and the bio has to sell — a
reader deciding whether to hire does not need his sports history in the first 250 words. It
stays here because it is the strongest human material the brand owns, and it belongs
somewhere on the site: a philosophy section, an interview, an about-the-process page.)*

## Conflicts to resolve

**⚠ Huia dates — three sources, three answers.**

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| `site/src/content/en.json` | 2015–2021 as Partner, *plus* a second overlapping entry 2013–2021 |
| `career/cv/cv-base.md` | 2015–2021 |
| `career/linkedin-apply/profile.py` | 2012–2021 |
| `RECRUITER-FAQ.md` (Caio's own words) | "nine years… joined from day 1" → implies 2012–2021 |

His own account and `profile.py` agree on nine years from the start. The site and CV say
six. If both are true, he joined around 2012 in one role and became partner around 2015 —
which would explain the site's two overlapping Huia entries, except their dates don't line
up either. **Needs Caio's answer, then propagate to all four files.**

**⚠ Age.** The FAQ says 40, but it is undated legacy material. Not used in any copy above.
Confirm before publishing anywhere.

**⚠ How much of the Console Kit is his, personally?** The positioning says he writes code,
so a client may reasonably read the Console Kit as his code. The live site cites "6,000+
commits, 34+ contributors" — that is the project, not him. The bio above deliberately says
he *owned* the layer and calls the Kit *proof*, never that he wrote it. If he did write a
substantial share, the bio can claim more and should. If he directed it, the current
wording is the honest ceiling. **Needs his answer.**
